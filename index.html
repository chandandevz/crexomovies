What is Khanapara Teer? How It's Played & Its Legal Standing in India

Updated for 2026 · 8 min read

Khanapara Teer is one of the most well-known archery-based betting games of Meghalaya, drawing participants from across North East India every single day. If you've heard the term but never quite understood what it actually involves, this guide breaks down the game, how the numbers are generated, and where it stands legally.

What Exactly Is Khanapara Teer?

Teer is a traditional archery-based lottery game that originated in Meghalaya and is now closely associated with Shillong and Khanapara (Guwahati, Assam). Unlike typical lottery draws that rely on machines or random number generators, Teer results are determined by the number of arrows that hit a target during a live archery round.

The game combines an actual sporting activity — archery — with a betting system built around predicting the count of arrows that land on the target. Because of this unique format, Teer is often described as part sport, part game of chance.

How Is Khanapara Teer Played?

The mechanics of the game are fairly consistent across most Teer counters, including Khanapara. Here's a simplified breakdown:

1. Two Rounds Per Day

Khanapara Teer typically runs two rounds daily — a First Round (F/R) and a Second Round (S/R) — at fixed times in the afternoon and evening.

2. Archers Shoot at a Target

A group of licensed archers shoot a set number of arrows (often around 30–50, depending on the round and venue) at a cylindrical target from a fixed distance within a short time window, usually just a few minutes.

3. The Count Becomes the "Number"

Once the shooting stops, officials count the total number of arrows that successfully hit the target. The last two digits of this total number become the winning result for that round. For example, if 782 arrows hit the target, the number "82" is declared the result.

4. Players Bet in Advance

Before each round, players place bets at licensed counters or through agents, choosing numbers between 00–99. Common bet types include:

  • Direct number — betting on the exact two-digit result.
  • House number — betting on the first digit of the result.
  • Ending number — betting on the second digit of the result.

Payout multiples vary depending on the type of bet and the counter's own rate list, but direct number bets typically carry the highest payout since the odds of guessing correctly are lowest.

Good to know: "Common numbers" or "dream numbers" circulated online and in local groups are informal guesses shared by players — they are not official predictions, are not verified, and have no guaranteed accuracy.

Legal Aspects of Khanapara Teer

This is where things get nuanced, since Teer occupies a distinct legal category compared to most forms of gambling in India.

Regulated Under a Specific Act

Teer is legally recognized and regulated in Meghalaya under the Meghalaya Amusements and Betting Tax (Amendment) Act, which governs archery-based betting activity within the state. Licensed Teer counters operate under this framework and are required to pay applicable taxes.

Khanapara's Position (Assam)

Khanapara itself is located in Guwahati, Assam, and functions largely as a major counter/booking point tied into the broader Teer ecosystem that originates from Meghalaya's licensed archery ranges. Betting laws and enforcement can differ from state to state, and Assam's own gambling regulations are separate from Meghalaya's Teer-specific legislation.

Why Is It So Popular in North East India?

Part of the game's appeal lies in its blend of tradition and chance — archery has deep cultural roots in Meghalaya, and Teer has evolved into both a livelihood source for archers/organizers and a daily ritual for thousands of participants who follow results, discuss patterns, and exchange informal number tips within their communities.
